This paper establishes conditions under which (NDU, see Qi (2024)), a framework for modeling dynamic decision-making, satisfies the Universal Approximation Theorem. NDU represents preferences via a continuous-time stochastic process governed by a Forward-Backward Stochastic Differential Equation (FBSDE), with latent preference state dynamics parameterized by continuous-time Residual Networks (ResNets). We prove two key results: (i) ResNet-parameterized aggregator functions within NDU can approximate regular functions in L^p spaces; (ii) NDU can approximate "Well Functions" uniformly over weakly compact sets of admissible consumption processes, extending to the full space via a diagonalization argument. These results demonstrate NDU's capacity to represent, and thus potentially endogenize within a unified framework, a broad class of dynamic preferences.
